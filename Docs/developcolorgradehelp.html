Color Grade

What it does

Color Grade tints the image by tonal range: one colour into the shadows, another into the midtones, a third into the highlights, and a fourth over everything. It is the modern form of split toning, and it is how most "looks" are actually built — cool shadows and warm highlights, or the reverse, in amounts small enough that you notice the mood and not the tint.

The controls

The Shadows, Midtones, Highlights and Global checkboxes do not adjust anything: they choose which range(s) the wheel and the Luminance slider below them write to. Check two and one drag moves both by the same amount. Global tints every pixel whatever its tone, which is the quickest way to lay a uniform wash over the picture.

The wheel is the adjustment. Each range has its own dot; the angle is the hue and the distance from the centre is the strength. Drag a dot out and that range takes on that colour; drag it back to the middle and the range is untouched. Double-click the wheel to return the checked ranges to neutral.

The Luminance slider under the wheel brightens or darkens the checked range(s) — the "and slightly lift the shadows" half of a grade, without going back to the tone panel. It reads the first checked range and writes all of them.

Blending and Balance

These two shape the windows the three ranges occupy, and they are panel-wide rather than per-range:

  • Blending (default 50) is how much the windows overlap. Low values give three distinct zones with visible transitions; high values smear them into each other so the grade reads as one continuous drift from shadow colour to highlight colour.
  • Balance (default 0) slides the split between the ranges. Push it right and the highlight colour claims more of the image; pull it left and the shadow colour does.

The defaults reproduce the fixed split the panel used before these existed, so leaving them alone is a perfectly good answer.

How to use it

  • Work small. A grade that is obvious as colour has usually gone too far; the effect you want is usually visible only in the before / after.
  • Set the tone first. The ranges are defined by tone, so re-doing exposure or the curve afterwards moves the grade around underneath you.
  • Complementary hues in shadows and highlights (teal / orange, blue / yellow) separate the picture into planes; the same hue in both is a wash and reads like a film stock.
  • Use Show Color Grade changes in the section's [⋮] menu to A / B it — the effect is subtle enough that the eye adapts to it within seconds.

Color Grade is not Calibrate

Calibrate re-points the primaries of the working space and holds neutrals neutral: it changes what red means. Color Grade deliberately does tint neutrals — that is the effect. Different jobs; both are available, and they stack.

Where it sits in the pipeline

Grading runs after the tone curve, on the rendered image, because the ranges it targets are the tones you can see rather than the scene's linear values.

What is saved

Hue, saturation and luminance for each of the four ranges, plus Blending and Balance, are part of the develop recipe, stored per scope in the sidecar — so a grade can be global or confined to a mask. They travel with presets, Copy / Paste Settings, History and multi-image editing. Which range checkboxes are ticked is UI state and does not travel.
