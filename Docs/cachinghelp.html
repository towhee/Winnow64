Caching: Thumbnails and Previews

Winnow keeps copies of your pictures so that browsing stays fast. None of them changes your image files or your edits, and every one can be rebuilt.

What Winnow keeps

Thumbnails. The small pictures in the grid and the film strip. Winnow reads them from each file (most cameras embed one) and keeps a copy in its thumbnail cache, so a folder opens at once the second time you visit it. Its limit is Preferences > Caching > Thumbnail cache size.

Developed thumbnails. When you edit an image, a small thumbnail of the edited picture is saved in the image's sidecar (its .xmp file), beside your edits. The grid then shows your edits wherever the file goes.

The image cache. Full-size pictures held in memory for the images around the one you are looking at, read ahead in the direction you are moving. This is what makes the next image appear at once. It is emptied when you leave the folder. Its size is Preferences > Caching > Image cache size.

Developed previews. Full-size pictures saved on your computer, in Winnow's own folder rather than beside your files. When an image has one, Winnow shows it instead of opening the file, which is much faster for raw files and large TIFFs. Its limit is Preferences > Caching > Developed preview cache size.

Develop also keeps the image you are editing in memory at full precision while you work on it. That is not something you need to manage.

Which images get a developed preview

Edited images, always. Without one, browsing would show the image without your edits until you opened it in Develop again.

Raw files with a small built-in preview. When browsing, Winnow shows the preview your camera built into the raw file. Some cameras build in a small one (many Sony cameras use 1616 pixels), which looks soft on a large screen. For those, Winnow renders the raw file itself and keeps the result.

Files that are slow to open. A file that takes a long time to open, such as a large 16-bit TIFF, is kept as a preview the first time Winnow opens it, so later visits are quick. It is the same picture as the file, with the file's wide color kept, and it is shown whether or not edits are shown.

Raw files whose built-in preview is already large, and ordinary JPEGs, do not need one: they already browse quickly.

Previews for the thumbnails on screen

When you pause, Winnow makes previews for the thumbnails you can see, starting with the ones nearest the current image.

It waits about a second and a half after you stop moving, and starts only once the images around the current one are ready to show. It stops the moment you select another image or scroll.

It also stops when the computer is busy: when memory is short, when the computer is running hot, or when Winnow is slow to respond. It then waits longer before trying again.

It never changes the picture you are looking at. A new preview is used the next time you go to that image.

A green circle on a thumbnail means its preview is ready.

Turn it on or off with Preferences > Develop > Build previews for thumbnails on screen. It is on unless you turn it off.

Building more at once

Preferences > Develop > Build developed previews in background makes previews for the whole folder, or the whole Library, using the same rules. On a large folder this takes hours, so it is off unless you turn it on.

Develop > Build Developed Previews makes previews now for the selected images, or for the thumbnails on screen. It includes every raw file, whatever the size of its built-in preview.

Camera cards

Previews are made in the background only for folders that are part of your Library. If you have not set up a Library, they are made for folders on your computer's own drives.

Camera cards are skipped: their files move when you import them, so a preview made before the import would be thrown away.

When the preview cache is full

When the developed preview cache is nearly full (90% of its limit), Winnow tells you once. It shows the limit, how much is used, how much free space the disk has, and a larger limit to choose if the disk has room for it.

From then on, the previews made in the background pause, so they do not remove previews you already have.

Previews saved while you edit, and Develop > Build Developed Previews, still go in. To make room, the previews you have not looked at for the longest time are removed.

A removed preview loses nothing. Your edits are stored with each image, and the preview is made again the next time it is needed.

Develop > Clear Developed Preview Cache deletes every developed preview.
