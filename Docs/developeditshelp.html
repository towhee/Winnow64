Edits

What it does

Edits is the top of the Develop dock, and it answers one question: what do the sliders below apply to? Every image starts with a single scope called Global, which covers the whole frame. Add a mask and you get a second scope — Mask 1 — with its own complete set of Basic, Curves, Color, Calibrate, Color Grade and Effects values, applied only where the mask says.

Selecting a scope in the dropdown is what points the panels below at it. The section headers do not change; their values do.

The Edits bar

One line carries the whole scope control:

  • The dropdown lists every scope on the image — Global, or Mask 1, Mask 2 ... , which you can rename to something meaningful (Sky, Face, Foreground). Picking one makes it active, and everything below the bar then edits it.
  • [+] adds a new mask.
  • The eye shows or hides the selected scope's whole contribution — a one-click before / after of everything in it, with no values lost.
  • The [⋮] menu carries the actions for that scope: modify its mask (M), reset it, rename it, delete it. Global cannot be renamed or deleted and has no mask; its menu offers New mask (N) and Reset Global. It also carries Reset all edits — which wipes the entire recipe, every scope, the crop and the spot heals, plus the history and the sidecar record, on every selected image, after a confirmation.

Scopes stack

Scopes are applied in stack order: Global first, then each mask on top of the result. Two masks that overlap both act in the overlap, in order. So the usual shape of an edit is a global rendering that is right for the frame as a whole, then masks that fix what the frame as a whole could not.

Masks

A mask scope is built from one or more submasks — gradients, a brush, colour and luminance ranges, and the AI selections (Subject, Sky, Background, Depth, Object) — combined in the Submasks list below the bar. Press N for a new mask, M to modify the active mask's, and O to show or hide the mask overlay on the image. The Submasks band has its own help page.

Multiple images

Develop edits apply to the whole selection, not just the image on screen: adjust a slider with several thumbnails selected and the same change lands on all of them. Masks are the exception — a mask belongs to the pixels of one image, so it never travels. That is also why a preset or Copy / Paste Settings always merges onto scope Global.

Related

  • History dock — the session's snapshots of this image's recipe.
  • Presets dock (P) — saved recipes, merged onto the active scope.
  • Copy / Paste Settings (Cmd/Ctrl+Opt+C, Cmd/Ctrl+Opt+V) — the same mechanism as a preset, unnamed.

What is saved

The whole scope list — names, enabled flags, every scope's values and every mask's submasks — is the develop recipe, written to the image's sidecar. The original file is never modified.
