Edits

What it does

Edits is the top of the Develop dock, and it answers one question: what do the sliders below apply to? Every image starts with a single scope called Global, which covers the whole frame. Add a mask and you get a second scope — Mask 1 — with its own complete set of Basic, Curves, Color, Calibrate, Color Grade and Effects values, applied only where the mask says.

Selecting a scope in the dropdown is what points the panels below at it. The section headers do not change; their values do.

The Edits bar

One line carries the whole scope control:

  • The dropdown lists every scope on the image — Global, or Mask 1, Mask 2 ... . Picking one makes it active, and everything below the bar then edits it.
  • To rename a mask to something meaningful (Sky, Face, Foreground), type over its name in the dropdown and press Enter (Esc cancels). Global cannot be renamed, and no mask can be named Global.
  • [+] adds a new mask: pick a mask tool from the menu and the mask is made with it, named Mask 1, Mask 2 ... (type over the name in the dropdown to rename it).
  • The eye shows or hides the selected scope's whole contribution — a one-click before / after of everything in it, with no values lost.
  • The [⋮] menu carries the actions for that scope: modify its mask (M), reset it, rename it, delete it. Global cannot be renamed or deleted and has no mask; its menu offers New mask (N) and Reset Global. It also carries Reset all edits — which wipes the entire recipe, every scope, the crop and the spot heals, plus the history and the sidecar record, on every selected image, after a confirmation.

Hide global image

Hide global image, in the same [⋮] menu (from any scope), shows only what your masks cover: everything outside every mask becomes transparent, so the loupe shows its background there. It is a tick in the menu; choose it again to bring the image back.

The masked areas look exactly as they do in the full picture, Global's adjustments included. A mask with no adjustments of its own still shows its area, and a mask whose eye is closed is hidden along with everything else. A pattern on a mask stays visible where it lays its mirrored copies.

Like the eyes, it is part of the image's recipe: it is saved with the image and an export follows it. A format with transparency (PNG, TIFF) keeps the hidden part transparent; a JPEG shows it as black.

Above:below sliders

Above:below sliders, in the same [⋮] menu, and in the menu you get by right-clicking an empty part of the Develop panel, puts each slider's name and value on one line with the slider full width below them. Choose it again to go back to name, slider and value on one line.

It applies only to the Develop panel. Until you use it, the Develop sliders follow the preference User Interface > Controls > Above/Below sliders. Once you have used it, the Develop sliders keep their own layout, and the preference changes only the other panels.

Scopes stack

Scopes are applied in stack order: Global first, then each mask on top of the result. Two masks that overlap both act in the overlap, in order. So the usual shape of an edit is a global rendering that is right for the frame as a whole, then masks that fix what the frame as a whole could not.

Masks

A mask scope is built from one or more submasks — gradients, a brush, colour and luminance ranges, and the AI selections (Subject, Sky, Background, Depth, Object) — combined in the Submasks list below the bar. Press N for a new mask, M to modify the active mask's, and O to show or hide the mask overlay on the image. The Mask band has its own help page.

Multiple images

Develop edits apply to the whole selection, not just the image on screen: adjust a slider with several thumbnails selected and the same change lands on all of them.

Most masks are the exception — a brush stroke or a gradient belongs to the pixels of one image, so it never travels. Subject, Sky and Background masks do: the model finds the subject or the sky in each image itself, so adding one with several images selected adds it to all of them, and the adjustments you make in it land on all of them too. A preset or Copy / Paste Settings always merges onto scope Global.

Related

  • History dock — the session's snapshots of this image's recipe.
  • Presets dock (P) — saved recipes, merged onto the active scope.
  • Copy / Paste Settings (Cmd/Ctrl+Opt+C, Cmd/Ctrl+Opt+V) — the same mechanism as a preset, unnamed.

Last global (bottom of the Develop panel) applies the Global settings of the last image you edited — plus its crop, level, warp, spots and lens settings — to the selected images. Masks are not copied.

Last + masks applies the last edited image's whole recipe, masks included. Subject, Sky and Background masks are redrawn for each image; brush, gradient and object masks land where they were on the source image.

Both are one undoable History step per image. Hover a button to see which image the settings come from.

What is saved

The whole scope list — names, enabled flags, every scope's values and every mask's submasks — is the develop recipe, written to the image's sidecar. The original file is never modified.
