Calibrate

What it does

Calibrate re-points the three primaries of the working colour space. Each of Red, Green and Blue gets a hue shift and a saturation scale, and the three results become the columns of a 3×3 matrix that is applied to every pixel. In effect you are telling Winnow “this camera's red is a little more orange than I want”, and the whole image responds to that new definition of red.

The matrix is built so that grey stays grey. Whatever you do to the primaries, a neutral pixel comes out neutral — calibration re-points colour without tinting the image or shifting the white balance.

The one deliberate exception is Tint, whose whole job is to tint the shadows, greys included.

The controls

In the combined Color panel this is the RGB mode. Every slider runs from −100 to +100 with 0 meaning no change, and double-clicking a slider returns it to 0. Click a slider and the arrow keys nudge it one unit at a time.

  • Tint (the first slider) adds green (left) or magenta (right) to the shadows only. It is the fix for a camera whose blacks lean green or magenta while the midtones are right. Brightness is held steady, and the effect fades out above the shadows.
  • Then a Hue and Saturation pair for each primary, red, green and blue from the top. The slider colours show which primary a pair belongs to.
  • Hue shifts that primary's hue, up to ±30° at full scale. The slider's colours show which way it goes: red moves toward magenta or orange, green toward yellow or cyan, blue toward cyan or purple.
  • Saturation scales that primary's saturation. +100 doubles it and −100 removes it. Brightness is held steady, so this is a colour control, not a second exposure.

The section's [⋮] menu has Reset, and its eye is a before/after that keeps your values.

Why the sliders interact

Move red toward green and you will see green's own response drop. That is correct, not a defect. Holding white fixed while red picks up a green component means something has to give, and a real change of primaries behaves the same way — Lightroom's Calibration sliders interact for exactly the same reason. What does survive is direction: a primary you have not touched keeps pointing where it did.

Where it sits in the pipeline

Calibration runs in linear light, before the tone curve, because re-pointing primaries is a change of colour space and belongs upstream of anything perceptual. That is why it feels different from the Color panel's Hue and Saturation, which run after the tone curve and act on the pixels you can already see.

Calibrate is not the Color panel's Red / Green / Blue

The Color section's Red, Green and Blue sliders are per-channel linear gains, folded into the white balance — a cruder second white balance that does move neutrals. Calibrate changes what the primaries mean and leaves neutrals alone. Different maths, different place in the pipeline; both are available.

When to use it

  • The camera's colour is off in a way white balance cannot fix. Skies are cyan-ish, foliage too yellow, skin too orange — but the greys are already neutral. That is a primaries problem, and this is the panel for it.
  • Matching two cameras. Shoot the same scene on both, neutralise each with white balance, then nudge the primaries of one until they agree. Save the result as a preset and apply it to every file from that body.
  • Deepening or opening up a colour globally. Pulling blue's saturation up enriches skies and shadows together, more naturally than a global saturation push, because it works on the definition of blue rather than on every colour at once.
  • A look. Small hue shifts on all three primaries are how a lot of film emulations get their character, and being upstream of the tone curve they stay coherent through highlights and shadows.

Work in small amounts, and judge it on a neutral reference or a colour target if you have one. Because everything downstream inherits the new primaries, calibration is worth setting before the tone and colour work, not after.

What is saved

The seven values (the shadows tint, and hue and saturation for each primary) are part of the develop recipe, stored per scope in the sidecar alongside every other adjustment, so they travel with presets, Copy/Paste Settings, History and multi-image editing like anything else. Masked scopes have their own Calibrate section, so a calibration can be confined to a mask.
