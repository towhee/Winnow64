Lens

What it does

Lens corrects what the lens did to the picture: distortion (straight lines that bow), chromatic aberration (red and blue edges that drift apart toward the corners), vignetting (corners darker than the centre) and colour fringing along high-contrast edges.

It can also do what a fast lens would have done: Lens Blur blurs the photo by depth, so the subject stays sharp and the background melts away.

The corrections are applied to the whole image before anything else, so every mask, AI selection and scope works on the corrected picture. That is also why they appear only in the Global scope: a mask cannot have a different lens. A mask scope's Lens section has just one slider, which refines the Lens Blur (see below).

Profile, Fringe and Blur

The radio buttons at the top of the panel choose what it shows: Profile (distortion and vignetting, with a lens profile or by eye), Fringe (chromatic aberration and colour fringes) or Blur (Lens Blur). Each shows only its own controls; the others keep their settings.

A button reads Profile * (or Fringe *, Blur *) when anything under it is off its default, so you can see an edit that is not showing. The section's [⋮] menu resets just the showing one, or the whole section.

Profile: lens profile

A lens profile is a measurement of one lens model, made by photographing test charts. Winnow uses the profiles of the lensfun project, which cover about 1,500 lenses and 1,000 cameras.

  • Lens profile turns the profile correction on. It is off by default for JPEGs, which the camera has usually corrected already. Preferences > Develop > Apply lens profile on raw files turns it on for every raw file you have not set yourself.
  • Lens shows which profile is used. Auto finds it from the lens named in the photo's EXIF and shows the match (“Auto: Nikon AF-S Zoom-Nikkor 24-70mm f/2.8G ED”) or why there is none:
    • no lens in EXIF — a manual or adapted lens, or a camera that does not record it.
    • no profile found — the lens is not in the database.
    • profiled on a smaller sensor — the only profile was measured on a smaller sensor than your camera's, so it does not cover your corners.
    • no focal length in EXIF — a zoom needs the focal length.

    Open the list to pick the lens yourself. It lists every profiled lens that fits your camera's mount. A picked lens also turns the profile on.

  • Distortion and Vignetting set how much of each correction is applied: 100 is the profile as measured, 0 is none, 200 is twice as much. A row is greyed out when the profile has no such measurement for this shot. Vignetting also needs the aperture in the EXIF. Hover over the Lens row to see what the profile corrects.

Correcting distortion moves the edges of the frame, so the picture is scaled just enough that no empty corners show.

Profile: manual

Manual corrections are set by eye and need no profile. Use them when the Lens row says no profile found (a lens the database does not have yet), on a JPEG, or to fine-tune a profile. When the profile is on they are applied on top of it.

  • Distortion (-100 to +100): plus straightens barrel distortion (lines bowing outward), minus straightens pincushion (lines pinched inward). The frame is scaled so no empty corners show.
  • Vignetting (-100 to +100): plus brightens dark corners, minus darkens them. This is the lens's own falloff, corrected before any crop. Effects > Vignette is the creative one, applied to the cropped picture.
  • Midpoint (0 to 100) sets how far into the frame the vignetting correction reaches: low values reach most of the picture, high values only the corners.

Fringe: chromatic aberration and fringing

  • Remove CA re-aligns red and blue with green. When the lens profile is on and measures chromatic aberration, that measurement is used. Otherwise Winnow measures it from the photo itself. Preferences > Develop > Remove chromatic aberration on raw files turns it on for every raw file you have not set yourself.
  • Purple fringe and Green fringe remove the coloured rim that appears along high-contrast edges anywhere in the frame, such as branches against a bright sky. Purple covers cyan, blue, purple and magenta. Only edge pixels whose colour has a fringe hue are changed, so a real coloured edge is left alone.

Blur: Lens Blur

Lens Blur imitates a wide aperture. An AI model estimates how far away every part of the photo is, and each part is blurred by how far it is in front of or behind the focus. The model is downloaded the first time you use Lens Blur (about 50 MB).

Like a real lens, the blur respects what is in front: a sharp subject does not smear into the blurred background behind it, while a blurred foreground spreads over what is behind it.

  • Amount is how strong the blur is, like opening the aperture. 0 is off.
  • Focus point: click the target button, then click the photo where it should be sharp. That sets Focus to the depth of the point you clicked. If Lens Blur was off, this turns it on.
  • Focus is the depth kept sharp, from 0 (the nearest part of the photo) to 100 (the farthest). The default, 0, keeps the nearest things sharp, which suits most portraits.
  • Focal range is how much depth around the focus stays sharp. Raise it to keep a whole group of people sharp.
  • Bokeh is the shape a blurred point of light takes: Circle (a modern lens wide open), 5 blades and 6 blades (a lens stopped down a little), Cat's eye (round in the middle and clipped toward the corners, like many fast lenses), Soap bubble (a bright rim) and Anamorphic (tall ovals).
  • Boost brightens the blurred highlights, so street lights and sparkles bloom into bright discs instead of dim grey ones.
  • Show depth shows the depth estimate instead of the photo: near is light, far is dark, and what stays sharp is tinted green. It is only a view and is not saved.

The depth estimate is very good but not perfect. When it gets something wrong, make a mask over it (a brush, Select Subject, an object) and use that mask's Lens blur slider: minus keeps the masked area in focus, plus blurs it. It does nothing while the Global scope's Lens Blur is off, and the panel says so.

Lens Blur is applied after everything else in the develop and before the crop. The Detail panel's 1:1 view does not show it.

Working with the controls

  • Double-click a slider to return it to its default (100 for the profile's Distortion and Vignetting, 0 for the Manual sliders and the fringe sliders, 50 for Midpoint).
  • The section's eye button shows the picture without any Lens corrections, keeping your settings. Its [⋮] menu has Reset Profile (or Fringe, or Lens Blur: whichever is showing) and Reset Lens.
  • The header reads Lens * when anything in the section is off its default.

What is saved

Every setting here is part of the develop recipe in the sidecar, except Show depth. The Lens profile, Lens manual corrections, Remove chromatic aberration, Defringe and Lens blur items travel with presets and Copy / Paste Settings (under Global settings); a mask's refine travels as Lens blur refine in the Lens section. They are recorded in History, and they apply to the whole selection when several images are selected.

Adding or updating profiles

The lensfun database is built into Winnow. To add a lens, or to use a newer database, put lensfun XML files in the lensfun folder of Winnow's application data folder (on macOS ~/Library/Application Support/Winnow/lensfun; on Windows %APPDATA%\Winnow\lensfun). A lens or camera defined there replaces the built-in one with the same name. Restart Winnow to load them.
