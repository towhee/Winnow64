Point Color

What it does

Point Color changes one particular color in the image and leaves the rest alone. You pick the color by clicking it in the image, then shift its hue, saturation and luminance. It is the Point Color half of Lightroom's Color Mixer.

A sampled color is more specific than a hue band. It is a hue and a saturation and a brightness, so you can warm one skin tone without touching an orange wall of the same hue, or deepen one blue in a sky without moving the blue of a shirt.

Sampling a color

Click the eyedropper, then click the color in the image. Winnow averages a small patch around the click, adds a swatch for it and selects it. The eyedropper puts itself away after each sample (press Esc to put it away without sampling). You can sample up to eight colors.

Click a swatch to select it: the controls below always edit the selected color. To delete one, select it and click the × at the right of the swatch row, or right-click its swatch for Delete Point and Delete All Points.

Winnow samples what you see on screen, so sample with your other adjustments in place. If an existing point has already shifted a color, sampling that same spot again picks up the shifted color.

The color field

The large panel is the neighbourhood of the selected color. Hue runs across it, with the sampled hue in the middle. Saturation runs up it, from grey at the bottom to vivid at the top. The narrow strip beside it is luminance.

The small ring marks the color you sampled. The large ring marks where your shifts are taking it. Drag the large ring in the field to shift hue and saturation together, or in the strip to shift luminance. The bar underneath shows the color before and after.

The sliders

  • Hue Shift, Sat. Shift and Lum. Shift are the field's three directions as numbers. Drag the field and the sliders follow, or move a slider and the ring moves.
  • Variance changes how much the selected colors differ from each other. Move it left to pull them together toward the sampled color, which evens out blotchy skin or patchy foliage. Move it right to spread them apart.
  • Range sets how far from the sampled color the change reaches. Move it left for only the closest matches, or right to take in more of the image. The three indented sliders under it (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) adjust one direction each. For example, raise Luminance range to catch the same color in shadow and in light.
  • Visualize Range shows what the selected color is changing. Affected pixels stay in color and everything else turns grey, so you can see the range before you shift anything. It is a view only. It is not saved, and it switches itself off when you pick up the eyedropper.

Greys are never selected. A color with almost no saturation has no hue to match on, so the change fades out as a pixel approaches neutral.

The section's [⋮] menu has Show Point Color changes (a before/after that keeps your values) and Reset Point Color (removes every point).

Where it sits in the pipeline

Point Color runs after the Color panel's adjustments and before Color Grade. It measures colors as they look on screen, through the current tone mapping, which is why the color you click is the color that gets selected. Like the other adjustments it works on masks too: sample and shift inside a mask to change a color in just that area.
