Point Color

What it does

Point Color changes one particular colour in the image and leaves the rest alone. You pick the colour by clicking it in the image, then shift its hue, saturation and luminance. It is the Point Color half of Lightroom's Color Mixer.

A sampled colour is more specific than a hue band. It is a hue and a saturation and a brightness, so you can warm one skin tone without touching an orange wall of the same hue, or deepen one blue in a sky without moving the blue of a shirt.

Sampling a colour

Click the eyedropper, then click the colour in the image. Winnow averages a small patch around the click, adds a swatch for it and selects it. The eyedropper puts itself away after each sample (press Esc to put it away without sampling). You can sample up to eight colours.

Click a swatch to select it: the controls below always edit the selected colour. To delete one, select it and click the × at the right of the swatch row, or right-click its swatch for Delete Point and Delete All Points.

Winnow samples what you see on screen, so sample with your other adjustments in place. If an existing point has already shifted a colour, sampling that same spot again picks up the shifted colour.

The colour field

The large panel is the neighbourhood of the selected colour. Hue runs across it, with the sampled hue in the middle. Saturation runs up it, from grey at the bottom to vivid at the top. The narrow strip beside it is luminance.

The small ring marks the colour you sampled. The large ring marks where your shifts are taking it. Drag the large ring in the field to shift hue and saturation together, or in the strip to shift luminance. The bar underneath shows the colour before and after.

The sliders

  • Hue Shift, Sat. Shift and Lum. Shift are the field's three directions as numbers. Drag the field and the sliders follow, or move a slider and the ring moves.
  • Variance changes how much the selected colours differ from each other. Move it left to pull them together toward the sampled colour, which evens out blotchy skin or patchy foliage. Move it right to spread them apart.
  • Range sets how far from the sampled colour the change reaches. Move it left for only the closest matches, or right to take in more of the image. The three indented sliders under it (Hue, Saturation, Luminance) adjust one direction each. For example, raise Luminance range to catch the same colour in shadow and in light.
  • Visualize Range shows what the selected colour is changing. Affected pixels stay in colour and everything else turns grey, so you can see the range before you shift anything. It is a view only. It is not saved, and it switches itself off when you pick up the eyedropper.

Greys are never selected. A colour with almost no saturation has no hue to match on, so the change fades out as a pixel approaches neutral.

The section's [⋮] menu has Show Point Color changes (a before/after that keeps your values) and Reset Point Color (removes every point).

Where it sits in the pipeline

Point Color runs after the Color panel's adjustments and before Color Grade. It measures colours as they look on screen, through the current tone mapping, which is why the colour you click is the colour that gets selected. Like the other adjustments it works on masks too: sample and shift inside a mask to change a colour in just that area.
