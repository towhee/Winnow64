Develop Module

Overview

The develop module edits the image, changing the light, color and effects. The changes are parametric, like a recipe.

The image file is not changed; instead the develop recipe is saved in a sidecar file (same name as the image file, with the .xmp extension).

Global and masks

Every image starts with one scope called Global, which covers the whole frame. Edits made while Global is selected apply to the entire image.

To change only part of the picture, add a mask (press N, or [+] on the Edits bar). Each mask is a scope of its own, Mask 1, Mask 2 and so on, which you can rename (Sky, Face). It has its own complete set of Basic, Curves, Color and Effects values, applied only where the mask covers.

A mask is built from one or more submasks: a gradient, a brush stroke, a colour or luminance range, or an AI selection such as Subject or Sky. Each submask can add to, subtract from or intersect with the mask. Choose a scope in the Edits dropdown and the panels below edit that scope.

Scopes are applied in order: Global first, then each mask on top of the result. The Edits and Mask panels have their own help pages.

Curves

The Curves panel shapes tone directly, and works two ways.

Parametric draws the curve your Basic tone sliders are already making, over the image's histogram. Drag up or down inside one of the four bands — blacks, shadows, highlights, whites — and that band's Basic slider moves with you; double-click a band to set it back to zero. The three handles under the plot set where the bands begin and end. They are the same handles that sit under the histogram at the top of the Develop panel, so moving one moves the other.

Point is the free-form curve. Click anywhere on the curve to add a point, drag it to move it, and double-click a point to remove it. Double-click the background to start over. The two end points cannot move sideways, but you can drag them up and down to set the black and white points.

The channel list next to the mode chooses which curve you are editing. RGB is the main curve and affects everything — an S shape adds contrast, a lifted bottom-left corner gives the faded, matte look. Red, Green and Blue edit one channel on top of the RGB curve, which is how you tint the shadows one colour and the highlights another.

Editing more than one image

Develop edits apply to every selected image. If more than one image is selected a red banner appears at the top of the Develop panel telling you how many, because the other images are not on screen while you edit. Each adjustment is copied to the other images as an absolute value, so Exposure +0.5 means +0.5 on all of them, whatever they started from. Only settings that you actually change are copied — the rest of each image's recipe is left alone.

Masks and spot heals are specific to the image they were made on and are never copied by an adjustment. The exception is a Subject, Sky or Background mask: Winnow finds the subject or the sky in each image itself, so adding one with several images selected gives every selected image its own, and the adjustments you make inside it apply to all of them.

A crop or straighten is copied, proportionally, to every selected image. Paste Settings (and applying a preset) also apply to the whole selection, and those do carry the crop and spot heals, because you asked for that exact recipe.

Raw Files

The Raw panel, at the top of the Develop dock, chooses what is developed: either the raw data or the embedded preview image every camera saves in its raw files. These choices apply to the whole image, underneath Global and every mask.

If you select 'Raw' then you can select how to demosaic (convert raw data into pixels). If you choose Winnow to demosaic, then you can also run noise reduction on the raw data, which is much better than running noise reduction later in the develop pipeline when the pixels are already 'baked'.

Export

Press X (or Develop > Export Images) to write the selected images out with their develop settings applied. Every selected image is exported with its own recipe — masks, spot heals and crop included — not the recipe of the image you happen to be looking at.

The export dialog covers where the files go (a subfolder of the source folder, or any folder you choose), what they are called (a filename template plus a suffix, and what to do when the name already exists), the format, and the size.

Source chooses what is written: Developed (your edits applied) or Camera preview, the JPEG the camera embedded in a raw file, exactly as the camera rendered it. Camera preview is for raw files only; any other image in the selection is exported developed.

Beyond JPEG, PNG and BMP you can write TIFF — at 8 or 16 bits per channel, which is worth using when the image will be edited further — and, on macOS, HEIC. Turn on Copy metadata from the original to carry the camera, lens, date and copyright across; it costs about a fifth of a second per image.

Exporting a folder of raw files takes a while, so the export runs in the background: the progress bar counts the images and Stop ends the run, leaving the files already written in place.

Export presets

Save the settings in the export dialog as a named preset with New. An export preset remembers everything, including the destination folder, so Develop > Export with preset > your preset exports the selection immediately without opening the dialog at all. Use it for the exports you repeat — a web-sized JPEG, a full-size 16-bit TIFF for retouching.
