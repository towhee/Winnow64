Detail

What it does

Detail holds the two controls that fight over the same thing — fine detail. Sharpening puts edge definition back, Denoise takes speckle away, and every setting of one changes what the other should be. That is why they share a panel. Like every other section it is per scope, so either can be confined to a mask.

Judge this whole panel at 1:1 zoom. A fit-to-window view hides noise and hides the smearing and halos the cures introduce, so it will lie to you in both directions.

Sharpening

This is capture sharpening: it restores the acutance every demosaic costs you. Raw files land soft by nature and essentially always want some. Radius, Detail and Masking are sub-controls of Amount and do nothing until Amount is above zero.

  • Sharpening — the strength, 0–150.
  • Radius — the width of the edge that gets enhanced, in pixels, 0.50–3.00. Around 1.0 suits most detailed subjects; go higher only for soft subjects or soft lenses, because a wide radius is what produces visible halos.
  • Detail — how much of the finest structure is sharpened rather than treated as noise. Low suppresses the smallest amplitudes, which is what you want on a noisy or high-ISO frame; high passes everything through for maximum bite on clean, detailed files.
  • Masking — confines sharpening to edges. At 0 everything is sharpened, including flat sky and skin, where sharpening only amplifies noise. Raise it and smooth areas are progressively protected while real edges keep the full effect. On portraits and skies this is usually the difference between usable and unusable.

Radius is the one control measured in pixels

Every other spatial radius in Develop — Texture, Dehaze, the vignette, grain size — is a fraction of the image, so it looks the same on the editing proxy as at full resolution. Sharpening cannot work that way, because acutance is a property of the pixel grid itself: one pixel is one pixel whatever the image size.

While you drag, Winnow shows a scale-honest approximation on the proxy so the slider responds; a moment after you stop, the full-resolution render replaces it with the true result. The full-resolution render, viewed at 1:1, is the one to trust.

Sharpening is not Texture

Basic's Texture also makes detail look crisper, but the two work on completely different scales and do different jobs:

  • Sharpening works at about a pixel, on edges, and recovers acutance lost to demosaic. It is a technical correction.
  • Texture works roughly ten times wider, ungated, and changes detail contrast in skin, bark and fabric. It is a look.

They occupy separate bands, so using both is normal and they do not double up. If an image looks mushy, reach for Sharpening; if it looks flat, reach for Texture.

Denoise / Denoise Color

Two strengths, 0–100:

  • Denoise is luminance noise reduction — the speckle in a high-ISO frame. It preserves ratios between the channels, so it smooths grain without shifting colour. It also costs detail: every luminance NR is a trade, and the useful range is usually far lower than it looks at 100 %.
  • Denoise Color is chroma noise reduction — the blotchy red / green mottling in deep shadows. It blurs the opponent-chroma channels and leaves luminance exact, so it is much cheaper in detail than luminance NR. Push this one first.

This is not the same control as Denoise raw in the Raw panel. That one is a learned, pre-demosaic denoiser that runs on the sensor's CFA data at decode time, for the whole image, and it is the better answer when a raw file is genuinely noisy. The pair here is a plain image-space filter that works on any file and can be masked.

Working the two together

A reliable order on a noisy file:

  1. Zoom to 1:1 on a part of the frame with both detail and flat area in view.
  2. Set Denoise Color first — it is nearly free in detail.
  3. Add Denoise only until the speckle stops distracting, not until it is gone.
  4. Bring up Sharpening, then raise Masking until the flat areas go quiet again.
  5. Drop Detail if the remaining noise is still being sharpened.

Where it sits in the pipeline

Noise reduction runs first, before the tone and colour work, so nothing downstream amplifies noise you were going to remove anyway. Sharpening runs near the end, after the vignette (so darkened corners are not detail-boosted) and before grain (so film grain is never itself sharpened).

Working with the sliders

  • Double-click a slider returns it to its default (Radius 1.00, Detail 25, everything else 0).
  • Click a slider and the arrow keys nudge one unit, Page Up / Page Down ten.
  • The section's [⋮] menu has Show Detail changes (a before / after that keeps your values) and Reset Detail.
  • The header reads Detail * when anything in the panel is off its default.

What is saved

All six values are part of the develop recipe, stored per scope in the sidecar, and travel with presets, Copy / Paste Settings, History and multi-image editing.
