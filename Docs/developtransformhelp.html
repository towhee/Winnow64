Transform

What it does

The Transform panel holds the three edits that change the shape of the picture rather than its tones: the crop, the straighten and the perspective correction. Press R (or the rectangle button in the Develop action row) to open it; opening it arms the crop overlay on the image, and the panel is only shown while that session is live.

All three are applied last in the develop pipeline and none of them touches a pixel of the original: like every other develop edit they are a recipe entry, reversible at any time and only made real on export.

The three modes

The Crop / Level / Warp buttons down the left choose which one you are working on — one at a time, and the letters C, L and W switch between them whether the panel or the image has the focus. Each row carries that mode's own controls and its own reset.

Crop (C)

Drag the handles on the image to set the frame. The Aspect list constrains it: As shot leaves it free, and the usual ratios (1 x 1, 3 x 2, 4 x 3, 5 x 7, 4 x 5, 8.5 x 11, 16 x 9, 16 x 10) are there, along with any you add yourself with Add custom aspect... — a custom ratio is remembered and appears in the list from then on, and Edit custom aspects... renames or removes them.

  • The padlock (or A) holds the current ratio while you drag, so the frame can only change size, not shape.
  • The flip button (or F) swaps the ratio between landscape and portrait — 4 x 5 becomes 5 x 4 — without your having to find a second entry for it.

Level (L)

Two ways to straighten, and they are the same value: draw a line on the image along something that ought to be horizontal or vertical — a horizon, a door frame — and Winnow rotates the picture to match, or type the Angle directly, from -45 to 45 degrees. Drawing adds to the current angle; a typed angle replaces it.

Warp (W)

Perspective correction for the converging verticals a camera tilted up or down produces. Drag the four corners onto something that is rectangular in the real world — the face of a building, a window, a painting — then press Return or double-click inside the shape, and that quadrilateral is squared up, taking the rest of the picture with it.

The header buttons

  • [?] — this page.
  • [E] — the result toggle. On, the crop is committed and you see the cropped, straightened, rectified picture; off, you are back to the whole frame with the overlay on top of it. It is the quickest way to check a crop without giving up the ability to keep adjusting it.
  • [R] — reset: crop, straighten and perspective all back to the full frame, and the aspect, lock and flip back to neutral with them. The small reset on each mode's row clears just that mode's contribution.
  • [X] — close the panel, committing the crop on the way out (the same thing R and the action-row button do).

What is saved

The crop rectangle, the straighten angle and the perspective correction are stored with the image in its sidecar, as part of the develop recipe. They describe these pixels, so an adjustment applied across a selection never carries them — but Paste Develop Settings and applying a preset do, because there you asked for that exact recipe. The aspect list, the last aspect used, the lock, the flip and the selected mode are preferences rather than edits, and they persist between sessions.
