Transform

What it does

The Transform panel holds the three edits that change the shape of the picture rather than its tones: the crop, the straighten and the perspective correction. Press R (or the rectangle button in the Develop action row) to open it; opening it arms the crop overlay on the image, and the panel is only shown while that session is live.

All three are applied last in the develop pipeline and none of them touches a pixel of the original: like every other develop edit they are a recipe entry, reversible at any time and only made real on export.

Using the mouse

While the panel is open the pointer is a crop cursor. Drag anywhere on the image, inside the frame or out, to draw a new crop.

Drag a handle to resize the frame; the pointer changes when it is over one.

Hold Space and drag to move the picture under the frame — that is how you reposition a crop. The wheel zooms, as it always does in Develop.

Hold Cmd (Ctrl on Windows and Linux) and drag to draw a level line.

Hold Opt (Alt on Windows and Linux) and the frame turns red: drag a corner to pull it into a perspective warp.

Crop

Drag the handles on the image to set the frame, or drag elsewhere to draw a new one. The Aspect list constrains it: As shot leaves it free, and the usual ratios (1 x 1, 3 x 2, 4 x 3, 5 x 7, 4 x 5, 8.5 x 11, 16 x 9, 16 x 10) are there, along with any you add yourself with Add custom aspect... — a custom ratio is remembered and appears in the list from then on, and Edit custom aspects... renames or removes them.

  • Lock (or A) holds the current ratio while you drag, so the frame can only change size, not shape.
  • Flip chooses Landscape or Portrait (or X swaps them) — 4 x 5 becomes 5 x 4 — without your having to find a second entry for it.

Angle

Two ways to straighten, and they are the same value: hold Cmd (Ctrl on Windows and Linux) and draw a line on the image along something that ought to be horizontal or vertical — a horizon, a door frame — and Winnow rotates the picture to match, or type the Angle directly, from -45 to 45 degrees. Drawing adds to the current angle; a typed angle replaces it.

Warp

Perspective correction for the converging verticals a camera tilted up or down produces. Hold Opt (Alt on Windows and Linux) and drag the four corners onto something that is rectangular in the real world — the face of a building, a window, a painting — then press Return or double-click inside the shape, and that quadrilateral is squared up, taking the rest of the picture with it.

Effects > Patterns works on the cropped picture. While this panel is open the picture is shown without it, so you crop the real photo.

The header buttons

  • [?] — this page.
  • [E] — the result toggle. On, the crop is committed and you see the cropped, straightened, rectified picture; off, you are back to the whole frame with the overlay on top of it. It is the quickest way to check a crop without giving up the ability to keep adjusting it.
  • [R] — reset: crop, straighten and perspective all back to the full frame, and the aspect, lock and flip back to neutral with them. The small reset on the Aspect, Angle and Warp rows clears just the crop, the straighten or the perspective.
  • [X] — close the panel, committing the crop on the way out (the same thing R and the action-row button do).

What is saved

The crop rectangle, the straighten angle and the perspective correction are stored with the image in its sidecar, as part of the develop recipe. They describe these pixels, so an adjustment applied across a selection never carries them — but Paste Develop Settings and applying a preset do, because there you asked for that exact recipe. The aspect list, the last aspect used, the lock, the flip and the selected mode are preferences rather than edits, and they persist between sessions.
