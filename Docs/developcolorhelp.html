Color

What it does

Color holds the blunt, global colour controls: three channel gains, and four HSL adjustments that act on every pixel at once. Nothing here selects a colour or a tonal range — that is what Color Grade, Calibrate and the Color Range mask are for. Reach for this panel when the whole picture needs the same push.

Red / Green / Blue

These are per-channel linear gains, folded into the white-balance vector before anything else runs. In effect they are a second, cruder white balance: they scale a channel, so they do move neutrals — push Red and a grey card goes pink.

That makes them the wrong tool for "the reds in this picture are too orange". For that, use Calibrate, which re-points what red means and leaves neutrals alone. The gains are here for cast correction that Temp and Tint cannot reach, such as mixed lighting or an old scan.

Hue, Saturation, Luminance

  • Hue rotates every hue in the image. Small amounts shift the overall colour character; large amounts are a special effect.
  • Saturation scales colourfulness uniformly. −100 is fully grey; positive values push every colour, including the ones that were already strong.
  • Luminance raises or lowers overall brightness after the tone curve. It is not Exposure: exposure works in linear light and is the correct control for "the frame was a stop dark", while Luminance moves the rendered image.

Vibrance

Vibrance is saturation weighted toward the muted colours: colours that are already saturated move little, colours that are nearly grey move most. It is the safer of the two for people and skies — skin tones are moderately saturated, so they hold while a flat sky comes up. A common pairing is a healthy Vibrance with a small negative Saturation.

Working with the sliders

  • Double-click a slider returns it to its default.
  • Click a slider and the arrow keys nudge one unit, Page Up / Page Down ten.
  • The section's [⋮] menu has Show Color changes (a before / after that keeps your values) and Reset Color.
  • The header reads Color * when anything in the panel is off its default.

Which colour panel do I want?

  • A cast over the whole picture — Basic's Temp / Tint first, then this panel's Red / Green / Blue gains.
  • The camera renders a colour wrongly — Calibrate.
  • Colour by tonal range (warm highlights, cool shadows) — Color Grade.
  • One colour only (that shirt, those leaves) — a Color Range mask, then adjust inside that scope.

Where it sits in the pipeline

The RGB gains are applied with the white balance, in linear light at the front of the pipeline. Hue, Saturation, Luminance and Vibrance run after the tone curve, on the pixels you can already see — which is why they feel more direct, and less forgiving, than Calibrate's primaries.

What is saved

All seven values are part of the develop recipe, stored per scope in the sidecar, so the panel can act globally or be confined to a mask. They travel with presets, Copy / Paste Settings, History and multi-image editing.
