Replace

What it does

The Replace tools take things out of a photo by replacing them with what should be there: dust, blemishes, a stray twig, a wire, a leash, a whole object. Every repair is a recipe entry, like the rest of Develop: the original is never touched, and a repair can be removed at any time.

Click the Replace button in the Develop action row, or press S, and choose a tool from the menu. While a tool is on, S turns it off.

To turn the tool off, press Esc on the image, click the X on its panel, choose Turn off Replace tool in the Replace menu, or choose the checked tool again.

The five tools

Spot

Click a blemish. Winnow finds a matching area nearby and heals the spot from it. Best for dust and small skin or sensor marks.

Heal

Brush over a flaw. Winnow picks a source area and blends its texture into the tone around the flaw, so the repair takes on the local brightness and colour.

A white ring marks the source, joined to the repair by a dashed line. Drag the ring to take the texture from somewhere else. Hovering either end outlines the brushed area at both places, so you can line the source up.

Clone

Like Heal, but the source is copied exactly as it is, tone and all. Use it when the blend of Heal smears an edge or a pattern that must line up. Drag the source ring the same way.

Fill

For things that are long, thin or scattered: a wire across the sky, a twig, a leash. Paint over it in as many strokes as you need, then press Return or Enter. Winnow fills the painted area piece by piece, each piece from its own nearby source.

Inpaint

For a whole object. Paint over it completely, with a little margin, then press Return or Enter. The area is replaced by an AI (regenerative) fill. The first time, Winnow offers to download the fill model.

Cover the object entirely. Anything left showing at the edge can guide the fill back into the shape you are removing.

The tool panel

While a tool is on, its panel opens in the Develop Editor, under the histogram: Replace for Spot, Heal and Clone, Fill for Fill, and Inpaint for Inpaint. It says how to use the tool. Its X turns the tool off, and its eye shows or hides the repairs, like Show repairs.

Every panel has a Size slider for the brush. The mouse wheel or a two-finger drag over the image, and [ and ], change the same size, and the slider follows them. While you drag the slider, the brush circle shows in the middle of the image.

The other Develop Editor panels are greyed out while a tool is on. Turn the tool off to adjust the photo again.

The Fill and Inpaint panels list the keys: make as many strokes as you need, then press Return or Enter to replace the painted area, or Esc to cancel the painting.

Copying spots to other images

Sensor dust sits in the same place on every frame, so the spots you fix on one photo can be applied to the rest. With the Spot tool chosen, the Replace panel shows how many spots the image has and a Copy all spots button.

Click Copy all spots. A Paste all spots button appears. Select the images to fix, then click Paste all spots: the spots are added to every selected image. Each image heals each spot from its own surroundings.

Only repairs made with the Spot tool are copied. Pasting never adds a spot an image already has, so pasting twice is harmless. Each image gets one History step, so the paste can be undone. The copied spots are kept until you quit Winnow.

Spots land at the same place in the frame. A portrait photo and a landscape photo from the same camera will not line up with the same dust.

While painting

[ and ] (or a two-finger drag, or the panel's Size slider) change the brush size.

In Fill and Inpaint, hold Opt/Alt while painting to erase part of the painted area. Esc clears the painting before you commit it.

Each repair leaves a pin. Click a pin to remove that repair.

Esc turns the tool off.

Checking your work

Each repair is a History step named after its tool: Spot, Heal, Clone, Fill or Inpaint.

Uncheck Show repairs in the Replace menu to see the photo without any of its repairs; check it again to bring them back. Nothing is removed.
